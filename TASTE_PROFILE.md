# Taste Profile — Reading Digest

This document is the spec the AI curator scores candidate articles against.
Anything that scores high against this should feel like it belongs next to
the pieces below. Anything that scores low should be filtered out before
it ever reaches the site.

## What makes something a YES
- **Voice**: personal, warm, first-person essay — feels like one specific
  person thinking out loud, not an institution or a brand.
- **Substance**: actually explains a real mechanism — how the brain does
  a specific thing (why dreams recur, why time perception shifts, what
  neuroplasticity actually changes) — not just a mood piece with the word
  "neuroscience" sprinkled in.
- **Angle**: uses the science in service of something bigger — self-growth,
  meaning, how to live, "romanticizing" ordinary experience — rather than
  science for its own sake.
- **Craft**: has an actual narrative or structural idea (a metaphor, a
  story, a thread) rather than being a bulleted summary of facts.

## What makes something a NO
- Listicles ("5 neuroscience facts about X", "7 psychology tricks for Y")
- Generic productivity/self-help blog voice — corporate, motivational-poster
  tone, no specific person behind it
- Pure science journalism with no personal or self-growth angle (fine
  content, wrong publication)
- Clickbait framing not backed by real substance in the piece

## Blurb voice
Each pick's one-line blurb should read like a magazine caption — a short,
editorial, slightly stylish line that sells the piece — not like an AI
assistant describing an article. Think "Table of Contents" teaser copy in
a print magazine, not a summary.
